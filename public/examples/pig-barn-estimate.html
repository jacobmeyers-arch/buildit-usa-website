Scope of Work

Complete teardown and removal of the pig barn — a ~300 SF outbuilding with severe lean, rotted framing, and a failed roof. The structure has no repair value; full demolition with salvage recovery is the priced approach. The foundation stays in place for a separate earthworks scope.

  • Site clearing & access
    • Chainsaw and hand-clear brush to open all four faces of the structure
    • Dumpster staged at the driveway
  • Safety-sequenced structural demolition
    • Roof comes off first (the main beam is loaded and deflected — nobody works under it)
    • Walls demolished outside-in, then sills and floor structure down to the foundation
  • Material testing & protective work practices
    • Lab samples of the rolled roofing and siding before anything is disturbed
    • Painted surfaces handled with protective equipment, isolation, and outdoor work practices throughout
  • Interior cleanout — all contents, junk, and garbage removed
  • Salvage recovery — siding boards 6'+, barn-door track and hardware, slider and bead-board doors, butcher block table — pulled during demo, sorted and stacked on site
  • Debris removal — hand-carried to a 30-yard roll-off (two pulls priced), full disposal included
  • Demolition permit — Town of Ballston building department, filed and priced inside this estimate
  • Electrical safety — service to the building confirmed dead before demo; an electrician allowance is carried in case the feed is live
  • Final cleanup and debris disposal — footprint raked and leveled
Not in This Scope
  • Foundation removal and grading — separate earthworks scope; your machine operator is already lined up (typically $2,500–$6,000 if added later)
  • Asbestos abatement — this estimate assumes lab results come back negative. If positive, abatement is a specialty-firm scope, estimated $2,000–$5,000, priced separately before any demo
  • Brush and overgrowth clearing beyond the demo access corridor

Sequence of Operations
1.Permit & Lab Clearance
File the demolition permit with the Town of Ballston building department. Collect PLM samples of roofing and siding; no disturbance until results are back. (lines 12, 10)
2.Site Clearing
Chainsaw and hand-clear brush on rear and sides. Open access to all four faces. Stage dumpster at driveway. (line 1)
3.Utility Disconnect Verification
Confirm the electrical panel is dead — electrician disconnect/cap if the feed is live. Investigate the terra cotta drain stub (covered by contingency if active). No demo until utilities are confirmed safe. (line 11; drain under the contingency soft-cost line)
4.Protection Setup
Lead-safe site practice stood up before disturbance: poly isolation, PPE, hygiene station, outdoor handling throughout. (line 9)
5.Interior Cleanout
Remove all junk, garbage, and loose materials. Carry to dumpster. Set aside salvage items (butcher block table, doors). (line 2)
6.Roof Removal
Strip roofing first, then pull rafters. SAFETY: 3" header deflection means the beam is loaded and compromised. Remove roof load before touching walls. No one under the beam. (line 3)
7.Wall Demolition
Work outside-in. Pull 6'+ siding boards for salvage as they come off. Remove barn-door track and horseshoe hardware. Pull slider door. Demo remaining framing. (line 4)
8.Floor & Sill Demo
Remove sill plates and remaining floor structure down to the foundation. Foundation stays — separate earthworks scope. (line 5)
9.Debris Haul & Dumpster Loads
All structural debris carried 80 yards by wheelbarrow relay. Load both pulls; disposal under the soft-cost disposal line. (line 6)
10.Salvage Sort & Site Cleanup
Boards stacked and stickered, doors and hardware staged, butcher block set aside. Rake and level the footprint. (lines 7, 8)

Access & Logistics
  • 80-yard hand carry to the dumpster at the driveway — biggest labor multiplier on this job. No equipment path currently; action item 2 is the cost-saving lever.
